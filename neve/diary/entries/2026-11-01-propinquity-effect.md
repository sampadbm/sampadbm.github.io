*A Reddit post I came across, from u/Most-Gold-434 in r/sociallycharismatic. This is my summary of it, not my own experience.*

The author moved to a new city knowing no one, and found the usual advice ("join a club", "try a meetup") forced. So they picked one coffee shop and went every Saturday morning at the same time, alone, with no plan.

- **Weeks 1–2:** nothing happened.
- **Week 3:** the barista said "You're becoming a regular." The next week she remembered their order, and the week after that she asked their name.
- **Weeks 5–6:** the same person kept sitting in the corner. A nod one week, then "We keep picking the same spot" the next, which turned into a fifteen-minute conversation. They started sitting together on Saturdays.
- **Week 8:** the barista was introducing them to other regulars. Two of those turned into people they now text regularly. One invited them to a dinner party, where they met four more people.

Their conclusion: they built a social circle "from being a predictable presence in a space until the space absorbed me", not from apps or networking events.

## The idea

The **propinquity effect**: people tend to form bonds with whoever they encounter repeatedly, not necessarily whoever they click with instantly or whoever shares their interests. Proximity plus repetition leads to relationships.

Making friends as an adult is hard not because adults are less social, but because adult life no longer has built-in repeated proximity. School made you see the same people every day, and work does that for some people. Outside of that, you have to create the repetition yourself.

The advice: pick a place, go at the same time, become a regular, and let the space do the work. It is slower than a meetup app.
